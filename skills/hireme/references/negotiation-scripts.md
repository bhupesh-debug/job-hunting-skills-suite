# Salary Negotiation Scripts — Word-for-Word

Use in Step 6a. These are proven sequences, not frameworks.
Adapt tone slightly for industry: finance = more direct, consulting = more collaborative.

---

## PART 1: DEFLECTING THE SALARY QUESTION (Before Offer)

**Scenario**: Recruiter screen asks "What are your salary expectations?"

### Script A — Defer to the Range (Preferred)
> "I'm open to the right number for the role and level. I'd love to understand what the
> band looks like for this position first — can you share the range you're working with?"

*Why it works*: Forces them to anchor first. You negotiate against their number, not yours.

### Script B — Range with Upside Signal
> "Based on my research and the scope of the role, I'm targeting somewhere in the
> [low-end to high-end] range — but I'm flexible depending on the total package,
> including bonus, equity, and benefits. What does the band look like?"

*Use when*: You need to show you've done homework but don't want to anchor too precisely.

### Script C — The Bridge (for internal transfers or re-leveling conversations)
> "I want to make sure we're aligned on the scope before I anchor to a number. Once
> I understand how this role is leveled relative to [X], I can give you a more
> useful answer. What's the typical comp structure here?"

---

## PART 2: RESPONDING TO A VERBAL OFFER

**The rule**: Never accept or negotiate on the spot. Always take time, even if you want it.

### Immediate Response to Offer (don't say yes, don't negotiate yet)
> "Thank you — I'm genuinely excited about this opportunity and the team. I want to
> give this the consideration it deserves. Can I have until [2–3 business days] to
> review the full details?"

**Never say**: "That's less than I expected" in the moment. Wait until you've slept on it.

---

## PART 3: THE COUNTER-OFFER CONVERSATION

**Setup**: You've reviewed the offer. You want to negotiate base salary.

### Script: Confident Counter (No Apology)
> "I'm very excited about the role — after reviewing the full package, I was hoping
> we could get to [X] on base. Based on [my research / my current comp / the scope
> of the role], I think [X] reflects the value I'd bring. Is there flexibility there?"

**Key principles**:
- State the number clearly. Don't say "around X" or "somewhere near X."
- Give one brief reason. Not a list.
- Ask if there's flexibility. Don't demand. Don't ultimatum.

### Script: Counter When You Have Another Offer
> "I'm genuinely most excited about this role and this team. I do have another offer
> at [X] — I want to be transparent about that. If you're able to get to [X], I'm
> ready to commit today."

**Only use if true.** Never fabricate competing offers.

### Script: When They Say They Can't Move on Base
> "I understand — is there flexibility on [signing bonus / title / remote policy /
> performance review timeline / equity]? I want to make this work."

---

## PART 4: NEGOTIATING TOTAL COMPENSATION (Non-Salary)

When base is locked, shift to these levers in this order:

1. **Signing bonus** — easiest to move; doesn't affect ongoing payroll cost
   > "Would a [one-time signing bonus] be possible to bridge the gap?"

2. **Early performance review**
   > "Would you be open to scheduling a 6-month review with a comp adjustment tied
   > to performance milestones?"

3. **Title / level**
   > "Is there flexibility on leveling? Being brought in at [Senior X vs. X] would
   > matter both for my career trajectory and comp in future cycles."

4. **Remote / hybrid flexibility**
   > "How flexible is the team on remote days? That affects my total compensation
   > picture materially."

5. **Equity / RSUs** (for tech/startup roles)
   > "What's the vesting schedule and is there flexibility on the grant size?"

---

## PART 5: CLOSING THE NEGOTIATION

### Script: Accepting After Negotiation
> "This works for me. I'm excited to join and want to hit the ground running. I'll
> sign today if you can get me the updated letter."

### Script: Declining Gracefully (if negotiation fails)
> "I appreciate everything — I have a lot of respect for [company] and the team I met.
> Ultimately the comp gap was too significant for me to bridge right now, but I hope
> our paths cross again."

*Why it matters*: Industries are small. Declining respectfully keeps the door open.

## PART 6: PSYCHOLOGICAL TRIGGERS (Recruiter-Side)

### Social Proof (without name-dropping badly)
**Weak**: "I interviewed at Goldman and McKinsey too."
**Strong**: "I'm in final rounds with a few firms in this space — I wanted to prioritize
this conversation because the role scope here is most aligned with where I'm headed."

### Scarcity Signal
> "I have a deadline on another offer — I'd hate to let that drive my decision when
> this is clearly the more interesting role. Is there a way to accelerate the timeline here?"

### Commitment Lock-In
Ask interviewers questions that make them say positive things about you:
> "Based on what you've seen so far, do you have any concerns about my fit for the role?"

When they answer (and they often answer positively), it becomes harder for them to
reject you — cognitive consistency kicks in.

---

## PART 7: FINAL-STAGE FAILURE MODES

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Going silent after the interview | Send follow-up email within 24 hours referencing a specific conversation moment |
| Renegotiating after you verbally accepted | Never. This destroys trust instantly. |
| Revealing desperation ("I really need this job") | Project optionality even if you don't feel it |
| Over-negotiating a role at a firm you genuinely need | Know your BATNA — don't negotiate yourself out of an offer you'd take at any reasonable number |
| Ghosting after an offer to use it as leverage | Firms talk to each other. Be direct instead. |
